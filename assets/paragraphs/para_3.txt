Tally ERP is one of the most widely used accounting software packages in the world, and nowhere is this truer than in India, where it has become virtually synonymous with computerized accounting itself. Developed by Tally Solutions, a company founded in 1986 and headquartered in Bangalore, Tally evolved from a simple accounting program into a complete enterprise resource planning solution. For millions of small and medium businesses, the words "Tally" and "accounting" are practically interchangeable — when a shopkeeper says his accounts are "in Tally," everyone understands exactly what that means.
The journey of Tally is a story of continuous reinvention. What began as Tally 4.5, a DOS-based accounting package, grew into Tally 5.4, 6.3, and 7.2 before the landmark release of Tally 9. The real transformation came with Tally ERP 9 in 2009, which turned the software into a full-fledged business management tool covering inventory, payroll, taxation, and statutory compliance. In 2020, Tally Solutions launched TallyPrime, the successor to ERP 9, featuring a redesigned interface and smoother navigation — yet most businesses and accountants still affectionately call the entire family "Tally ERP," a testament to how deeply the product is embedded in everyday commercial life.
The core strength of Tally lies in the sheer breadth of functionality packed into a single, lightweight application. At its heart is the voucher entry system, where every transaction — payment, receipt, sales, purchase, journal, credit note, debit note — is recorded through simple, intuitive screens. From these vouchers, Tally automatically generates ledgers, registers, and the complete set of financial statements. The software handles multiple currencies, cost categories, cost centres, budgets, and scenario management, making it powerful enough for growing businesses while remaining simple enough for a first-time user with minimal training.
Beyond basic bookkeeping, Tally ERP excels in inventory management. It tracks stock items, stock groups, godowns (warehouses), batches, and expiry dates, and can value inventory using methods like FIFO, LIFO, and weighted average. Manufacturing features such as bill of materials and manufacturing journals allow small production units to manage their processes within the same software. Payroll processing, including attendance, salary structures, and statutory deductions, is also built in, sparing businesses the cost of separate HR systems.
